# Workflow 2: Infographic or poster

Produce a one-page poster or infographic, such as a programme overview, a
patient education sheet or a study recruitment poster.

## Before you start

You need:
- The exact content: title, key messages, dates, contacts, any numbers
- Size and use: A4 or A3, printed or on screen
- Brand rules: colours, logo, fonts, if your institution has them
- A text assistant. One that can produce a web page (HTML) or an image will
  let you finish inside the assistant. If yours cannot, take the content plan
  from step 2 into PowerPoint, Canva or your usual design tool.

**Recruitment posters for research:** the wording approved by your ethics
board is the wording you must use. Use AI for layout only. Do not let it
rewrite approved text, and submit the final poster for approval as usual.

## Step 1: Define the poster

```
I need a one-page [A4 portrait] poster.
Purpose: [e.g. recruit participants, explain a programme, summarise a guide].
Audience: [WHO], reading it [WHERE, e.g. on a clinic noticeboard from two
metres away].
The one thing they must take away: [MESSAGE].
The action I want: [e.g. scan the QR code, email, attend].

Here is all the content. Use only this. Do not add facts, statistics or
claims:
[PASTE CONTENT]

First, tell me what is missing or unclear. Do not design yet.
```

## Step 2: Content plan

```
Organise the content into a poster plan:
1. Headline of ten words or fewer
2. Three to five sections, each with a short heading and at most 25 words
3. One visual idea per section (icon, simple chart, timeline or number)
4. A clear call to action with a space for a QR code
Tell me which of my content you left out and why.
```

Cut until it fits. A poster that says everything says nothing.

## Step 3: Produce the layout

```
Create this poster as a single self-contained HTML page sized for
[A4 portrait] printing.
Style: [e.g. clean and modern, bright, institutional].
Colours: [BRAND COLOURS OR "choose a palette with strong contrast"].
Body text no smaller than [14] points. High contrast between text and
background. Leave a labelled empty box for the QR code and a space for the
logo. Do not invent a logo.
```

Open the page in your browser and print to PDF (set margins to none).
If your assistant makes images instead, ask for the same plan as an image and
check every word, because image generators often misspell text.

## Step 4: Refine

Give specific feedback, one round at a time:

```
Changes:
1. [e.g. The title is hard to read against the background. Darken it.]
2. [e.g. Increase all body text by two points.]
3. [e.g. Fit the page to exactly 21.0 cm by 29.7 cm.]
Change only these. Leave everything else as it is.
```

## Check before you use it

- Is every word, date, contact and number exactly as you supplied it?
- Can it be read from the distance it will be seen at?
- Does it meet accessibility basics: strong contrast, no meaning carried by
  colour alone, plain language?
- Are logos and brand colours used with permission?
- For research recruitment: is the text identical to the approved version,
  and has the final poster been approved?

## Where this came from

This is how I produce A4 programme posters and a study recruitment poster:
content first, then layout, then small specific corrections (contrast, font
