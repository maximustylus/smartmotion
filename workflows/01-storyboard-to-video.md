# Workflow 1: Storyboard to short video

Turn an idea into a short explainer or tutorial video by planning the story
first, then generating one short clip per scene.

## Before you start

You need:
- One sentence on what the viewer should do or understand after watching
- Who the viewer is, and where they will watch (clinic screen, phone, booth)
- Your real content: the steps of the procedure, the features of the tool, or
  the key messages
- A text assistant (any), a video generator that turns text into short clips,
  and a simple video editor to join clips and add captions

Know your video generator's clip length. Many produce clips of only a few
seconds, so a 60-second video is about ten clips. Check yours before step 3.

## Step 1: Define the video

```
I am a [YOUR ROLE] making a [LENGTH, e.g. 60-second] video.
Audience: [WHO, including age range and what they already know].
Where they watch it: [SETTING, and whether sound will be on].
After watching, they should: [ONE ACTION OR ONE IDEA].
Tone: [e.g. warm, calm, encouraging].
Language: [LANGUAGE].

Before writing anything, ask me up to five questions about what is missing or
unclear. Do not write the script yet.
```

## Step 2: Find the story

```
Here is my content:
[PASTE YOUR REAL CONTENT]

Propose three different story angles for this video. For each, give a
one-line premise and a three-act outline:
Act 1, the problem the viewer recognises.
Act 2, the discovery or the how-to.
Act 3, the result and the single next step.
Use only the content I gave you. Do not add facts.
```

Pick one angle. Change it until it sounds like you.

## Step 3: Storyboard

```
Using angle [NUMBER], write a storyboard of [NUMBER] scenes, each
[CLIP LENGTH] seconds, as a table with these columns:
Scene number | Time | What we see | On-screen text | Narration | Act

Rules: one idea per scene. On-screen text of seven words or fewer. The last
scene shows the call to action and where to go next. Assume some viewers watch
without sound.
```

Read it aloud against a timer. Cut scenes until it fits.

## Step 4: Write the clip prompts

```
Turn each storyboard scene into a prompt for a text-to-video generator.
For every scene, specify:
- Format: aspect ratio [16:9 or 9:16] and visual style [e.g. cinematic,
  flat illustration, screen recording style]
- Subject: who or what, with age, clothing and setting described so the
  audience recognises their own community
- Action: what happens in these [CLIP LENGTH] seconds
- Camera: shot size and one movement (for example slow push in)
- Lighting and mood
- Continuity: repeat the same description of any recurring character
Do not depict real, identifiable people. Do not include logos I have not
supplied.
```

Tip: run this step in a second assistant and compare. A first draft from one
tool often improves when another tool is asked to deepen it.

## Step 5: Generate and assemble

1. Generate each clip. Regenerate the ones that look wrong rather than
   accepting them.
2. Join the clips in order in your video editor.
3. Add captions, your narration or music, and the final call to action
   (link or QR code).
4. Keep colour and mood consistent: a common approach is cooler and quieter
   for the problem, warmer and brighter for the result.

For screen tutorials of your own app or system, record your real screen for
the how-to scenes and use generated clips only for the opening and closing.

## Check before you use it

- Is every fact and instruction in the video correct and current?
- Does any scene show a real person, a patient, or a setting that could
  identify someone?
- Do the people shown represent your audience respectfully?
- Is the video labelled as containing AI-generated footage?
- Are captions accurate, and is the text readable on a phone?
- Has it gone through your usual communications approval?

## Where this came from

This is the method I used for a 60-second community booth video for the NEXUS
health app: ten short clips in three acts (lost, discovery, momentum), with a
second assistant used to deepen the first storyboard.
