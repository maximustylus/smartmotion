# Workflow 4: Pitch slides

Build a short, persuasive deck for a proposal: a new programme, a piece of
equipment, a pilot or a grant idea.

## Before you start

You need:
- Who decides, and what they care about
- The single thing you are asking for
- Your real evidence: numbers, sources, costs, timeline
- How long you have to present
- A text assistant. If you have Copilot in PowerPoint, it can draft slides
  from an outline. If not, paste the outline into your slide tool by hand.

## Step 1: Define the pitch

```
I am pitching to [WHO, e.g. head of department, grant panel].
They care most about: [e.g. health outcomes, cost, manpower, risk].
My ask: [THE DECISION OR RESOURCE YOU WANT].
Time: [MINUTES] to present, then questions.

Here is my material. Use only this. Do not add figures or references:
[PASTE NOTES, DATA, COSTS]

Before drafting, tell me: what is the strongest part of this case, what is the
weakest, and what will they ask that I have not answered?
```

## Step 2: Storyline

```
Write the storyline as slide titles only, [NUMBER] slides at most.
Each title is a full sentence stating the point of that slide, so that the
titles read in order make the whole argument.
Order: the problem and who it affects, why now, the proposal, the evidence,
what it costs and what it needs, risks and how they are managed, the ask.
```

Read the titles aloud. If the argument does not hold with titles alone, fix it
here, not in the slides.

## Step 3: Build the slides

```
For each slide, give:
- Title (from the storyline)
- At most three supporting points of 12 words or fewer
- One suggested visual (chart, diagram, photo, number) and the data it needs
- Speaker notes of 60 to 80 words
- Source for every figure, taken from my material. If a figure has no source
  in my material, write "SOURCE NEEDED" instead of guessing.
```

Then move it into your slide tool and apply your institution's template.

## Step 4: Rehearse against the hard questions

```
Act as [DECISION MAKER], sceptical and short of time.
Ask me the five hardest questions about this pitch, one at a time. After each
of my answers, tell me what was weak about it.
```

## Check before you use it

- Is every number traceable to a source you have read yourself?
- Are costs complete: licences, staff time, training, maintenance?
- Does the deck fit the time, at roughly one to two minutes per slide?
- Is the ask on the final slide specific, with an amount and a date?
- Have "SOURCE NEEDED" markers all been resolved or removed?

## Where this came from

I use this structure for innovation and funding pitches: one ask, titles that
carry the argument, and a rehearsal against a sceptical reader before the real
one.
